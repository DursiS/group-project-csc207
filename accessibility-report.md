# Accessibility Report

## 1. Principles of Universal Design
*For each Principle of Universal Design, write 2-3 sentences — or point form notes — explaining which features of your program adhere to that principle. If you do not have any such features, you can either:*
- *(a) Describe features that you could implement in the future that would adhere to the principle, or*
- *(b) Explain why the principle does not apply to a program like yours.*

- Flexibility In Use: The design allows for a range of preferences in gameplay, through different times, topologies, and sides of the board to play on. So, people can play the way they prefer rather than in one fixed mode.

- Simple and Intuitive: Most chess rules are the same as standard, so the rules are intuitive, and any new topologies introduced are simple and communicated clearly to make it simple.

- Perceptible information: Communicates directly whose turn it is and whether their analysis metrics are being displayed.

- Size and Area: It applies, the UI is large enough to accommodate a diversity of visibility characteristics.

- Equitable Use: Not fully met yet; those who play would need to have access to a computer, Wi-Fi, not be visually impaired and be able to use a mouse to play. We could implement an offline mode, and audio Reponses to play and hear plays made in future to accommodate.

- Tolerance error: Largely handled by the inner layer, illegal moves are simply ignored, and a selected piece can be deselected by clicking it again, so a mis click never has a harmful effect.

- Low physical effort: As a digital game, it requires only a mouse and a few clicks per turn, so it demands very little physical effort.

## 2. Marketing
*Write a paragraph (3-6 sentences) about who you would market your program towards, if you were to sell or license your program to customers. This could be a specific category of users, such as "students", or vaguer, such as "people who like games". Try to give a bit more detail along with the category.*

We would market our program towards all chess players, but especially those bored of standard chess rules wanted to play with new topologies or any enjoyer. We can market the program to such a wide audience because of low hardware and Wi-Fi necessities. The built-in position analysis and game play-back also make it a useful learning tool, giving players at every skill level feedback on their moves.

## 3. Demographics
*Write a paragraph about whether your program is less likely to be used by certain demographics. Your discussion here should be informed by the content of our embedded ethics modules this term.*

Less likely to be used by those visually impaired or physically impaired enough to not be able to use a mouse, as the program currently doesn't support any sound indications of moves or the board, and no alternative controls to move pieces. Under the social model of disability, it is not the impairment itself that excludes these users; it’s our program’s lack of accommodations that turns those impairments into limitations. Adding those features (audio output, alternative controls) would remove these limitations.
